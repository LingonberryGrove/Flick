# Vision

## The problem

Deciding what to watch has become harder than watching itself.

Many people experience decision fatigue when trying to find
something to watch. What should be an enjoyable experience is
instead preceded by an exhausting process of scrolling through
catalogs or searching online without making a choice. The friction of
discovery often overshadows the activity itself, ending with the user
rewatching something familiar or giving up altogether.

This problem affects two main types of users:

- Someone at the end of a workday who wants a quick, low-effort
  suggestion for tonight. They give up on browsing before they find
  anything, or default to something familiar.

- Film enthusiasts who have developed a distinct taste over time and
  find that mainstream recommendations no longer reflect it. They want
  a system that genuinely recognizes and builds on their preferences
  rather than serving the same popular titles regardless of what they
  actually enjoy.

Both need a system that respects their time and their taste.

## Market and similar solutions

**IMDb** is a comprehensive database, but discovery there requires
you to already know what you're looking for. It's a reference, not a
recommender.

**Streaming platforms** have recommendations built in, but they're
biased toward platform-owned content and biased toward what everyone
else watches. The infinite scroll UI amplifies decision fatigue
rather than reducing it.

**FLIK** (Rocket Digital Apps) is the closest conceptual competitor
(also swipe-based) but hedges by including trending lists, text search,
and an AI chat. The multiple discovery paths dilute the core
interaction, and personalization stays surface-level rather than
building a meaningful user profile over time.

Flick commits to swipe-first discovery and profile-driven
personalization as the whole product, not one option among many.

## How users solve this problem today

Most users default to asking friends for suggestions, browsing forums,
using heavy filters, or scrolling through multiple streaming platforms
at once. These approaches are time-consuming, socially dependent, and do
not adapt to individual taste in any structured way.

## What Flick must do

- Let someone find something to watch without manually browsing
  catalogs
- Improve recommendations over time based on real interactions,
  not just declared preferences
- Guide toward a decision fast enough that discovery doesn't
  displace viewing
- Maintain a user profile that reflects individual taste and
  engagement, and lets the user see and shape it
